# Price test — Standard multi-integration HITL builds

**Do not raise prices until prerequisites clear.** Treat current $1,500 Standard / Lead Qualifier price as acquisition/proof pricing.

## Prerequisites (all required)

From productized-offers pricing ladder + sellability plan:

1. **≥3 paid Lead Qualifier deliveries** closed and handed off (same shape, not three unrelated customs)
2. **Proof bar met** — see [PROOF-COLLECTION.md](./PROOF-COLLECTION.md) (public LQ Loom and/or authorized outcomes)
3. Delivery hours known: median hours per LQ deal logged below
4. Demo/intro conversion healthy enough that you are not starving pipeline (directional: &gt;25% of qualified intros → deposit is the ladder’s guide, not a guarantee)

## Test design (after 3× LQ)

| Item | Spec |
|------|------|
| **What changes** | Quote multi-integration HITL builds (Lead Qualifier with 2+ systems, or Advanced-shaped work) at **$2,500–$3,000** |
| **What stays** | Focused $1,000 for single-connection jobs; existing $1,500 page copy until the test cohort finishes |
| **Cohort** | Next **5** qualified intros that fit multi-integration HITL |
| **Success** | ≥2 deposits at the new band **or** ≥3 verbal “price is fine” with 1 deposit |
| **Failure** | 0 deposits and ≥3 explicit “too expensive vs Zapier/Clay” → hold at $1,500, improve proof/boilerplate, retest later |
| **Decision** | Success → update `lib/marketing.ts`, `components/offer.tsx`, and offer pages to the new Standard/Advanced numbers. Failure → keep $1,500; do not raise Advanced in isolation |

## How to quote during the test (live calls)

1. Confirm shape is multi-integration HITL (not a $1,000 Focused job).
2. Say: “For this scope we’re quoting **$2,500–$3,000** fixed—written scope, deposit, definition of done.”
3. Anchor vs DIY: Clay/Zapier monthly stack + founder time vs one owned workflow.
4. Log outcome in PIPELINE-TRACKER (offer fit + price quoted + won/lost reason).

## Anti-patterns

- Raising site prices before 3 LQ deliveries
- Testing $5k+ without proof assets
- Apologizing for the price or reverting mid-proposal without a written change
